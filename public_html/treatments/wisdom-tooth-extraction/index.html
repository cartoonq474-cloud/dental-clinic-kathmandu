Clinical Anatomy

Types of Impacted Wisdom Teeth

When the human jaw lacks space, third molars become trapped in abnormal positions beneath the gum and bone.

Mesioangular Impaction

The tooth tilts forward toward the front of the mouth, pushing directly against the adjacent second molar root.

↔️

Horizontal Impaction

The tooth lies completely on its side deep in bone, causing chronic pressure headaches and jaw decay.

⬆️

Vertical Impaction

The tooth is upright but unable to break through thick overlying bone and fibrous gum tissue.

Partial Pericoronitis

A flap of swollen gum traps food debris and bacteria, causing recurring painful infection, trismus, and bad taste.

Clinical Indications

Why You Shouldn't Delay Wisdom Tooth Removal

Ignoring an impacted third molar can lead to severe structural dental damage.

Severe Jaw Pain & Trismus

Inability to open the mouth fully, throbbing headaches, and painful swallowing radiating to the ear.

Neighbor Molar Decay

Food trapping between the impacted tooth and second molar creates deep cavities below the gumline.

Dentigerous Cysts

Fluid-filled sacs form around unerupted crowns, hollowing out surrounding jawbone and damaging nerves.

Orthodontic Crowding

Eruptive forward pressure pushes front teeth out of alignment, undoing years of braces treatment.
