Biological Causes

Why Does Jawbone Depletion Occur?

Understanding why bone loss happens and how advanced oral surgery regenerates solid foundations.

⏳

Long-Term Missing Teeth

Without natural tooth roots to stimulate bone, the jawbone shrinks by up to 50% within the first 12 months.

Sinus Pneumatization

The maxillary air sinus in the upper jaw expands downward over time, leaving paper-thin bone above the teeth.

Periodontal Bone Loss

Chronic gum infections and tartar buildup dissolve the supportive alveolar bone holding natural teeth.

Traumatic Extractions

Rough tooth extractions damage the thin outer bone plate, causing rapid ridge collapse during healing.

How Bone Grafting Regenerates Living Bone

Bone grafting does not simply fill a hole—it utilizes osteoconductive biomaterials that act as a microscopic mineral scaffold. Over 3 to 4 months, your body's own osteoblast cells migrate into the scaffold, depositing fresh collagen and calcium to replace the graft with 100% natural living bone.

This creates a dense, vascularized foundation that permanently anchors titanium implants with a 98.9% lifelong success rate.

Clinical Protocol

Our 5-Step Guided Surgical Workflow

A sterile, painless oral surgical process ensuring rapid cellular regeneration and zero infection.

01

3D CBCT Bone Density Mapping

We analyze high-resolution 3D radiographic scans to measure bone height, width, and sinus cavity dimensions down to the sub-millimeter.

02

Gentle Anesthesia & PRF Preparation

Under profound local anesthesia, a small vial of your blood is centrifuged to harvest autologous Platelet-Rich Fibrin (PRF) growth factors.

03

Delicate Membrane Elevation

Our Oral Surgeon gently elevates the delicate Schneiderian sinus membrane without tearing, creating a sterile space for bone placement.

04

Bone Matrix Placement & Collagen Barrier

Sterile Swiss Bio-Oss particles mixed with PRF are densely packed and protected with a resorbable collagen barrier membrane.

05

Biological Healing & Implant Integration

Over 3 to 4 months, the graft turns into dense living jawbone, ready for solid, lifelong dental implant placement.

Bone Grafting & Sinus Lift FAQs

Expert answers on bone regeneration and maxillary sinus floor elevation in Nepal.

The maxillary sinus is an air-filled cavity located above your upper back molars. When teeth are lost, the sinus expands downwards and the jawbone shrinks. A sinus lift gently elevates the sinus membrane and places bone graft granules underneath to create vertical bone depth for implants.

We use premium Swiss Bio-Oss® deproteinized bovine bone matrix and synthetic hydroxyapatite combined with platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) from your own blood to stimulate natural bone regeneration.

Socket preservation bone grafting starts from NPR 15,000–25,000. Indirect (crestal) sinus lifts cost NPR 25,000–35,000, while complex direct (lateral window) sinus lifts range from NPR 35,000–60,000 depending on biomaterial volume.

Do NOT blow your nose forcefully for 2 weeks, sneeze with your mouth open to avoid sinus pressure spikes, avoid air travel or scuba diving for 10 days, and take prescribed decongestants.

Bone graft biomaterial takes approximately 4 to 6 months to completely vascularize and remodel into dense, living bone capable of supporting lifetime dental implant chewing loads.

Bone graft rejection is exceptionally rare (<1%). Premium biomaterials like Bio-Oss® are completely sterilized and biocompatible, serving merely as a physical scaffold that your own body replaces with natural bone.
