What Is a Dental Bridge?

A dental bridge is a fixed restorative treatment used to replace one or more missing teeth. It works by placing custom crowns on the healthy teeth on either side of the gap (called abutment teeth), which support a false replacement tooth (called a pontic) suspended between them. This bridge is permanently cemented in place, completing your smile and restoring chewing function.

Who Needs a Dental Bridge?

A bridge is an effective, non-surgical alternative to dental implants for replacing missing teeth. You should consider a bridge if you have:

  • One or two missing teeth in a row.
  • Difficulty chewing foods or speaking due to tooth loss.
  • Surrounding teeth that are shifting or tilting into an empty space.
  • Facial shape changes or cheek sagging caused by missing teeth.

Benefits of Dental Bridges

  • ✓ Restores function: Restores full ability to chew, bite, and speak.
  • ✓ Prevents tooth shifting: Keeps surrounding teeth aligned in their correct positions.
  • ✓ Non-surgical: Completed in two clinical visits without requiring bone surgery.
  • ✓ Rejuvenates look: Supports your lip and cheek structures to prevent sagging.

Step-by-Step Bridge Procedure

  • 1

    Abutment Shaping

    The dentist shapes the support teeth on both sides of the empty space to prepare them for the bridge crowns.

  • 2

    Impression Take

    We take high-resolution dental impressions of the shaped teeth and empty space to send to the lab for custom bridge fabrication.

  • 3

    Temporary Bridge Fit

    A temporary acrylic bridge is fitted to protect your prepared support teeth while the lab crafts your custom permanent bridge.

  • 4

    Final Cementation

    Once the custom bridge is ready, we verify the fit and bite alignment, then permanently cement the bridge in place.

Recovery & Aftercare

Maintain standard brushing habits. You must use specialized floss threaders or interdental brushes to clean the space underneath the suspended false tooth (pontic) to keep your gums healthy and prevent decay on support teeth.
