Dual Mechanical Strength: Metal + Porcelain

After a root canal, the tooth loses internal moisture and blood circulation, becoming fragile like dry wood. Human molars endure over 150–200 pounds of pressure during daily chewing.

A PFM (Porcelain-Fused-to-Metal) crown surrounds the tooth like a cast-iron reinforcement, absorbing heavy chewing shocks through its metal core while presenting a natural tooth-colored ceramic exterior.

Clinical Protocol

Our 5-Step PFM Crown Procedure

A reliable, accurate restorative workflow ensuring airtight seal and chewing comfort.

01

Tooth Preparation & Margin Definition

Under local anesthesia, the tooth is shaped with a smooth circumferential chamfer to allow adequate space for metal and ceramic.

02

Precision Impression & Shade Match

We take high-definition impressions of the prepared tooth and record your exact enamel shade using the VITA guide.

03

Temporary Protective Crown

A comfortable temporary cap is placed over your tooth to protect it from thermal sensitivity while the lab casts your crown.

04

Metal Casting & Porcelain Firing

The dental lab casts the Co-Cr coping and fuses multiple layers of aesthetic ceramic in a high-temperature vacuum furnace.

05

Permanent Cementation & Bite Polish

We check the bite alignment, verify airtight margins, and permanently bond the crown with high-strength dental luting cement.

Frequently Asked Questions

PFM & Metal Crowns FAQs

Facts about Porcelain-Fused-to-Metal and Full Cast Metal crowns in Nepal.

A PFM crown features a strong cobalt-chromium metal alloy inner core coated with tooth-colored porcelain on the exterior, combining metal structural strength with natural tooth aesthetics.

PFM and full metal crowns are ideal for back chewing molars where biting forces are highest and cosmetic visibility is less critical.

Standard PFM crowns cost NPR 6,000–9,000 per tooth. High-noble nickel-free CAD/CAM PFM crowns cost NPR 9,000–14,000. Full cast metal crowns cost NPR 5,000–8,000.

PFM crowns utilize standardized metal casting and layering techniques, making them a time-tested, budget-friendly option for cost-conscious patients.

Avoid biting down directly on hard metal caps, bones, or ice cubes. Brush twice daily and floss around the crown margins to maintain healthy gum attachments.
